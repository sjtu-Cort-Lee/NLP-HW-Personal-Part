The old library was quiet after midnight. A student opened a notebook and copied
a paragraph from a public domain novel, not for training a model, but for testing
whether a language model could keep enough context while using a smaller cache.

The passage was intentionally short. It mentioned a letter, a journey by train,
and a conversation that returned to the same names several times. Repeated names
help a cache policy reveal whether it keeps early sink tokens, recent local
tokens, or a small set of important middle tokens selected by attention scores.

This tiny file is only a smoke-test substitute for PG-19. It is not a benchmark
sample, and any numbers produced from it should be reported as smoke-test
results rather than final experimental conclusions.
